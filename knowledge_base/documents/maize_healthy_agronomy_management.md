# Best Agronomic Practices for Maintaining Healthy Maize Crops in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa

## 1. Overview of Healthy Maize Cultivation in KP
Maize (*Zea mays*) is the second most widely cultivated cereal crop in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, planted as both a spring crop (February–June) and a kharif/autumn crop (July–November). Achieving optimum grain and silage yields requires integrated management of seedbed preparation, certified hybrid selection, balanced split fertilizer application, precision irrigation, and systematic pest and weed monitoring. Healthy maize leaves exhibit a uniform, deep emerald-green color, smooth linear-lanceolate leaf blades, parallel veins without chlorosis, and complete freedom from necrotic spots, rusty pustules, or insect chewing damage.

## 2. Morphological Characteristics of Healthy Maize Canopy
- **Foliage Inspection:** Leaves should be broad, upright, turgid, and completely green from the leaf base to the tip. Ear leaves (the leaf subtending the primary cob) and adjacent upper leaves must remain fully green and photosynthetic through the physiological dent stage.
- **Root and Stalk Vigor:** Thick, sturdy lower stalks with well-developed aerial brace roots anchoring the plant against monsoon lodging.

## 3. Seedbed Preparation, Certified Seed, and Planting Geometry
- **Land Preparation:** Deep chisel plowing followed by two cultivator passes and rotavation to produce a deep, well-aerated seedbed free of hardpans.
- **Certified Seed Procurement:** Use certified seed of high-yielding open-pollinated synthetic varieties (Jalal, Azam, Pahari) or approved commercial single-cross hybrids. Seed must have a minimum germination rate of 90%.
- **Ridge Planting:** Plant on raised ridges spaced 70–75 cm apart, with plant-to-plant spacing of 20–25 cm (achieving 55,000–65,000 plants per hectare). Ridge planting is critical in KP to prevent root waterlogging and collar rot during heavy monsoon rainstorms.
- **Seed Treatment:** Treat certified seed with an approved fungicidal-insecticidal dressing (such as Imidacloprid + Tebuconazole) prior to sowing to prevent early wireworm, shoot fly, and seedling blight attacks.

## 4. Balanced Soil Fertility and Split Nutrient Management
- **Soil Requirements:** Thrives in deep, fertile, well-drained sandy-loam to clay-loam soils with a pH between 6.5 and 7.5.
- **Fertilizer Regimen (NPK Guidelines for KP):**
  - **Phosphorus (P2O5) and Potassium (K2O):** Apply full recommended dose (e.g., 90–120 kg/ha DAP and 60 kg/ha SOP) as a basal broadcast at final seedbed preparation. Potassium strengthens stalk walls and enhances drought/cold tolerance.
  - **Zinc (Zn):** Apply 15–20 kg/ha Zinc Sulfate (33% Zn) at sowing, especially in alkaline, calcareous soils common in Peshawar and Mardan valleys where zinc deficiency causes white bud chlorosis.
  - **Nitrogen (N):** Apply in three equal splits: 1/3 at sowing, 1/3 at knee-high stage (V6, 30–35 days after emergence), and 1/3 just before tasseling (VT, 50–55 days after emergence). Never apply all nitrogen at once to avoid leaching and volatilization losses.

## 5. Water Management and Critical Growth Windows
- **Irrigation Windows:** Maize requires 500–700 mm of water throughout its cycle. Furrow irrigation on ridges saves 25–30% water compared to flat flooding.
- **Critical Stages:** Moisture stress during silking (R1) and early blister (R2) causes asynchronous pollen shed and silk emergence, leading to barren cobs and severe yield reduction. Ensure soil remains moist during flowering and grain fill.

## 6. Integrated Weed and Pest Monitoring
- **Weed Management:** Keep fields weed-free during the critical weed-competition period (first 30–40 days). Apply an approved pre-emergence herbicide (such as S-metolachlor + Atrazine) within 24 hours of sowing on moist soil, or perform mechanical hoeing at V4–V6.
- **Fall Armyworm and Stem Borer Scouting:** Inspect leaf whorls weekly for pinholes, frass, and "window pane" feeding signs caused by *Spodoptera frugiperda* or *Chilo partellus*. Use pheromone traps and conserve larval parasitoids.

## 7. Relevant Crop-Stage Schedule
- **Sowing to Emergence:** Soil moisture check, ridge placement, seed treatment verification.
- **V4 to V6 (Knee-high):** First nitrogen side-dressing, inter-cultivation/earthing up, weed eradication.
- **VT to R1 (Tasseling/Silking):** Second nitrogen top-dressing, mandatory irrigation, ear-leaf inspection.
- **R5 to R6 (Dent to Black Layer):** Monitor grain maturity; cease irrigation when black layer forms at the kernel base.

## 8. Environmental Stewardship and Safe Farm Practices
- Calibrate spray nozzles accurately when applying crop protection chemicals.
- Do not apply synthetic fertilizers on waterlogged soils to minimize nitrate leaching into village aquifers.
- Safely dispose of empty pesticide containers by triple rinsing, puncturing, and burying away from water sources.
