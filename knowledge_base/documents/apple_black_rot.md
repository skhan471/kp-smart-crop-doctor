# Apple Black Rot and Frogeye Leaf Spot (Botryosphaeria obtusa): Identification and Integrated Control

## 1. Disease Identification and Overview
Black rot, caused by the fungal pathogen *Botryosphaeria obtusa* (anamorph *Diplodia seriata*), is a multi-phase fungal disease affecting apple leaves, branches, and fruit in the temperate fruit production belts of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. On foliage, the disease is commonly known as "Frogeye Leaf Spot." On woody tissue, it produces debilitating branch cankers, and on ripening fruit, it causes firm, dark concentric rot that renders fruit unmarketable.

## 2. Foliar, Branch, and Fruit Symptoms
- **Frogeye Leaf Spot:** Initial foliar symptoms emerge shortly after petal fall as small purple flecks on the upper surface of mature leaves. These spots enlarge to circular lesions (4–7 mm in diameter). The center of each spot dies and turns light tan or grayish-brown, while the outer margin remains dark purplish, resembling the eye of a frog. Minute black pycnidia (spore-bearing fruiting bodies) may appear in the center of older lesions.
- **Canker Phase:** Sunken reddish-brown cankers develop on bark, especially around pruning cuts, winter-injury sites, or fire blight strikes. Cankers slowly girdle limbs, resulting in branch dieback.
- **Fruit Rot Phase:** Fruit lesions typically originate near the calyx end from infection early in the season or through insect wounds. The rot develops dark concentric rings of alternating brown and black bands. Decayed fruit eventually shrivel into persistent black mummies that cling to tree branches.

## 3. Causal Organism and Pathogen Biology
*Botryosphaeria obtusa* survives winter as pycnidia and perithecia in dead wood, pruned brush, bark cankers, and mummified fruit hanging in trees. In warm, rainy weather during spring and early summer (optimum temperature 24°C–27°C with wetting periods of 9 hours or more), conidia and ascospores are released and dispersed by rain splash and wind currents to infect foliage and developing fruitlets.

## 4. Prevention and Cultural Practices
- **Sanitary Pruning:** Sanitation is the single most effective control measure for black rot. Prune out all dead, dying, cankered, and fire-blight-damaged branches during the dormant winter period. Always cut at least 15 cm (6 inches) below visible canker margins into healthy wood.
- **Mummy Removal:** Thoroughly hand-pick and destroy all dried, mummified fruits clinging to limbs or resting on the orchard floor before spring bud break.
- **Brush Disposal:** Immediately remove and burn or deeply bury pruned wood. Leaving brush piles adjacent to the orchard generates immense spore reservoirs that re-infect trees throughout spring.
- **Minimizing Tree Stress and Wounding:** Maintain tree vigor through balanced soil nutrition and adequate irrigation. Prevent trunk and bark injury from machinery, frost cracking, and wood-boring insects.

## 5. Integrated Disease Management (IDM) Framework
1. **Dormant Clean-up:** Meticulous removal of cankered twigs, mummies, and dead wood.
2. **Bud Break to Petal Fall:** Protect foliage against early frogeye leaf infections using protective fungicides timed with primary spring infection rains.
3. **Summer Monitoring:** Scout foliage for frogeye leaf spots. Prune out sudden flag branches showing canker symptoms during dry weather.
4. **Insect and Hail Management:** Control codling moth and manage physical wounds that serve as infection courts.

## 6. Treatment and Control Recommendations
- **Chemical Control:** Fungicide programs designed for apple scab generally control the frogeye leaf spot phase if maintained through early summer. Protective fungicides such as Captan or Mancozeb applied at tight cluster, pink, petal fall, and early cover sprays provide strong preventative suppression.
- **Dormant Sprays:** A delayed-dormant spray of copper-based fungicides (Copper Oxychloride or Bordeaux mixture) helps sterilize superficial bark surfaces and reduce spore output from overwintered cankers.
- **Advisory Note:** Chemical fungicides cannot cure existing woody cankers; physical excision of infected branches is mandatory. Consult local KP extension agronomists for local registration status.

## 7. Relevant Crop-Stage Guidance
- **Dormant Period (Jan–Feb):** Canker pruning, removal of mummified fruit, brush burning.
- **Green Tip to Bloom:** Preventative sprays if orchard has a severe history of frogeye leaf spot.
- **Petal Fall to 4th Cover (May–July):** Key window for protecting fruit against calyx-end rot.
- **Post-Harvest:** Remove diseased windfall fruit before overwintering.

## 8. Safety, Pre-Harvest Intervals, and Environmental Notes
- Strictly adhere to the pre-harvest interval (PHI) for all protectant fungicides (typically 14–30 days for pome fruit).
- Always wear chemical-resistant gloves, respirators, and protective face shields when handling fungicide concentrates.
- Never spray during windy conditions or allow runoff into surface water streams in northern KP valleys.
