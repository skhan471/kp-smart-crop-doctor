# Apple Scab (Venturia inaequalis): Identification, Symptoms, and Integrated Management in KP

## 1. Disease Identification and Overview
Apple scab, incited by the ascomycete fungus *Venturia inaequalis* (anamorph *Spilocaea pomi*), is one of the most economically devastating foliar and fruit diseases in the temperate, high-altitude apple-growing valleys of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, specifically Swat, Chitral, Dir, and Abbottabad. During cool, wet spring seasons with prolonged leaf wetness, unchecked scab infections cause severe defoliation, stunted fruit growth, severe cracking, and substantial post-harvest losses.

## 2. Foliar and Fruit Symptoms
- **Leaf Symptoms:** Initial symptoms appear on the undersides of young expanding leaves near bud break as olive-green to dull yellowish, irregular circular spots. As lesions mature, they turn dark olive-green to black with a velvety or sooty texture due to conidiophore development. Leaf margins may become distorted, puckered, or curled, eventually turning chlorotic and dropping prematurely.
- **Fruit Symptoms:** Young infected fruit develop distinct olive-brown, crust-like lesions that become corky and scabbed over. Severe early fruit infections cause cracking, misshapen fruit, and premature fruit drop.
- **Differentiating Characteristic:** Scab lesions have diffuse, velvety margins in early stages and corky, cracked surfaces in later stages, contrasting with the concentric zonation of black rot.

## 3. Causal Organism and Pathogen Biology
*Venturia inaequalis* overwinters in dead fallen leaves on the orchard floor as pseudothecia. In spring, when temperatures reach 10°C to 20°C and leaves are wet for 9–24 continuous hours, mature pseudothecia forcibly discharge primary ascospores into air currents. Ascospores land on young leaves and fruit, initiating primary infection. Secondary cycles occur throughout the growing season via asexual conidia dispersed by rain splash and wind.

## 4. Prevention and Cultural Practices
- **Orchard Floor Sanitation:** The overwintering fungal inoculum resides entirely on fallen leaf debris. Flail mowing fallen leaves after leaf drop or applying a 5% agricultural urea spray to fallen leaves in autumn accelerates microbial breakdown of leaf tissue, reducing primary spring ascospore loads by up to 70–80%.
- **Canopy Aeration and Sunlight Penetration:** Prune apple trees annually during dormant winter months (January–February in KP) to open the canopy. Good air movement accelerates leaf drying, shortening wetting periods required for spore germination.
- **Irrigation Management:** Avoid overhead sprinkler irrigation. Use basin or drip irrigation to keep tree canopies completely dry.
- **Resistant Varieties:** In newly established orchards in Swat and Dir, plant resistant or partially tolerant apple cultivars recommended by ARI Tarnab.

## 5. Integrated Disease Management (IDM) Framework
Integrated Apple Scab Management requires coordinating sanitation, leaf-wetness monitoring, and targeted protective applications:
1. **Green Tip to Pink Bud Stage:** Primary risk period. Monitor temperature and rain duration. Apply protective fungicide sprays prior to anticipated rainfall if wetness exceeds Mills infection thresholds.
2. **Petal Fall to Cover Sprays:** Secondary conidial cycle. Maintain protective coverage if rainy conditions persist.
3. **Post-Harvest Sanitation:** Rake, compost, or shred fallen leaves before winter snowfall.

## 6. Treatment and Control Recommendations
- **Protective Treatments:** Protectant copper fungicides (e.g., Copper Hydroxide or Bordeaux mixture 1%) may be applied as a green tip dormant wash. During early green cluster to petal fall, protectant fungicides such as Mancozeb or Captan provide excellent barrier protection when applied prior to infection events.
- **Curative / Post-Infection Treatments:** Systemic triazole fungicides (such as Difenoconazole) or strobilurins possess post-infection kickback activity (within 48–72 hours of an infection event). Rotate chemical classes with different FRAC modes of action to prevent fungal resistance.
- **Regulatory Warning:** Always consult certified local Agriculture Extension Officers (Directorate General of Agriculture Extension KP) for currently registered commercial formulations and local seasonal schedules. Never exceed manufacturer label rates.

## 7. Relevant Crop-Stage Guidance
- **Dormant Stage (Winter):** Sanitation, fallen leaf urea spray, structural canopy pruning.
- **Green Tip to Tight Cluster:** First critical protective spray window to protect emerging green tissue.
- **Pink Bud to Petal Fall:** Primary ascospore discharge peak; monitor rainfall closely.
- **Summer Cover (Fruit Development):** Scout weekly; manage secondary conidial spreads.

## 8. Safety, Pre-Harvest Intervals, and Environmental Notes
- Wear complete Personal Protective Equipment (gloves, goggles, mask, protective suit) during spray application.
- Observe strict Pre-Harvest Intervals (PHI) of at least 14 to 30 days depending on the compound used.
- Avoid spraying during full bloom to protect honeybees and wild pollinators essential for fruit set. Do not spray during windy conditions to avoid pesticide drift into adjacent streams and irrigation channels.
