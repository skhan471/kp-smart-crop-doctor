# Common Rust (Puccinia sorghi) in Maize: Identification, Biology, and Management in KP

## 1. Disease Identification and Economic Significance
Common rust, caused by the obligate biotrophic basidiomycete fungus *Puccinia sorghi*, is widespread in both spring and autumn maize crops in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. The disease is favored by cool, humid conditions and often emerges early in the growing season. Severe early infections cause extensive foliar damage, leading to premature leaf chlorosis, accelerated desiccation, reduced ear weight, and stalk weakness resulting in lodging.

## 2. Foliar Symptoms and Diagnostic Features
- **Pustule Morphology:** Initial symptoms appear on both upper and lower leaf surfaces as small, chlorotic flecks. These rapidly develop into prominent, raised, oval-to-elongate cinnamon-brown or golden-brown pustules (uredinia).
- **Spore Release:** The leaf epidermis ruptures around the pustule margins, releasing massive clouds of powdery, reddish-brown urediniospores when rubbed with fingers.
- **Pustule Distribution:** Unlike Southern Rust (which occurs almost exclusively on the upper leaf surface), Common Rust pustules appear densely on both the upper and lower leaf surfaces, as well as on leaf sheaths and occasionally on husks.
- **Late Season Progression:** As the maize crop matures or temperatures rise above 28°C, the pustules turn dark brownish-black as the fungus switches to producing two-celled, overwintering resting spores called teliospores.
- **Differentiating Characteristic:** The powdery raised pustules breaking through the epidermis on both leaf surfaces clearly distinguish Common Rust from the flat rectangular necrotic lesions of Gray Leaf Spot and the broad elliptical lesions of Northern Corn Leaf Blight.

## 3. Causal Organism and Pathogen Biology
*Puccinia sorghi* is an obligate parasite requiring living host tissue. It cannot overwinter in crop residues in plains where freezing or dry winter conditions occur; instead, airborne urediniospores blow into KP maize fields from warmer southern regions (Punjab and Sindh) or overwinter on wild volunteer maize plants and alternate hosts (*Oxalis* species). Optimum infection conditions include moderate temperatures of 16°C to 25°C, high relative humidity (near 100%), and 6–8 hours of leaf wetness from night dews or morning fog.

## 4. Prevention and Cultural Practices
- **Genetic Resistance:** Planting resistant hybrid or synthetic maize cultivars (such as Azam, Jalal, Pahari, or Babar developed by CCRI Pirsabak) is the most reliable, cost-effective defense against common rust. These varieties carry single-gene (Rp genes) or general quantitative adult-plant resistance.
- **Planting Date Adjustment:** Early planting of spring maize (late February to early March in KP plains) and early planting of autumn maize (late July to early August) allows the crop to pass critical grain-filling stages before heavy spore flights arrive.
- **Crop Spacing and Field Sanitation:** Ensure proper plant spacing (20–25 cm plant-to-plant on ridges) to allow rapid canopy drying. Eliminate volunteer maize plants and wild broadleaf weeds along field borders.
- **Nutritional Balance:** Avoid excessive top-dressing with urea (nitrogen), as soft, lush foliar growth is substantially more susceptible to rapid rust pustule proliferation.

## 5. Integrated Disease Management (IDM) Framework
1. **Variety Selection:** Always prioritize certified CCRI/PARC-recommended rust-resistant seed.
2. **Canopy Monitoring:** Inspect mid-canopy leaves weekly starting at the knee-high stage (V6).
3. **Threshold Assessment:** Fungicide treatment is rarely needed on resistant hybrids. On susceptible cultivars, intervention is justified only if rust pustules cover more than 5% of leaf area on leaves below the ear before tasseling, with cool, humid weather forecast.

## 6. Treatment and Control Recommendations
- **Chemical Control:** If threshold levels are breached early in crop development (between V8 and VT), systemic fungicides such as Azoxystrobin, Pyraclostrobin, Propiconazole, or Tebuconazole provide protective and early post-infection suppression.
- **Spray Restriction:** Sprays applied after milk stage (R3) do not provide economic yield returns.
- **Official Guidance:** Only use fungicides approved and registered under the Agricultural Pesticides Ordinance of Pakistan. Consult local extension staff for authentic product identification to avoid adulterated chemicals.

## 7. Relevant Crop-Stage Guidance
- **Seedling to Knee-High (V1–V6):** Rare infection; monitor fields in cool foothill valleys (Swat, Abbottabad).
- **Tasseling to Silking (VT–R1):** Most vulnerable period; monitor ear leaves closely.
- **Milk to Dent (R3–R5):** Pustules transition to black teliospores; treatment is economically non-viable.

## 8. Safety, Pre-Harvest Intervals, and Environmental Notes
- Observe minimum 21- to 35-day Pre-Harvest Intervals before cutting for forage or harvest.
- Keep farm livestock off treated maize fields during spray withholding periods.
- Clean spraying equipment thoroughly away from village drinking wells and irrigation canals.
