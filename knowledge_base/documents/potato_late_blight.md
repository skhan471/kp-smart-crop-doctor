# Potato Late Blight (Phytophthora infestans): Biology, Foliar Symptoms, and Emergency Management in KP

## 1. Disease Identification and Destructive Potential
Late blight, incited by the oomycete pathogen *Phytophthora infestans*, is the single most destructive disease of potatoes worldwide and in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. It threatens both the autumn potato crop in the central plains (Peshawar, Nowshera, Charsadda, Mardan) and the high-elevation summer potato crops in Swat (Kalam, Miandam) and Hazara (Abbottabad, Naran). Under favorable weather conditions (cloudy, cool, damp weather), late blight can destroy an entire potato field within 7 to 10 days, causing total crop failure and devastating post-harvest tuber rot in transit and storage.

## 2. Foliar, Stem, and Tuber Symptoms
- **Foliar Lesions:** Initial signs appear as small, water-soaked, irregular pale-to-dark green spots, usually starting at leaf tips or leaf margins where free water collects. During humid weather, lesions expand with alarming speed, turning into large, dark purplish-brown to black necrotic areas.
- **Chlorotic Halos:** Active lesions are encircled by a distinctive diffuse pale green or yellowish chlorotic halo on the upper leaf surface.
- **Underside White Mildew Sporulation:** The hallmark diagnostic feature of late blight is a delicate, white, cottony or fuzzy mildew growth visible on the lower leaf surface, especially along the outer margins of the dark lesions during early morning hours or when relative humidity exceeds 90%.
- **Stem Blight:** Dark brown to black lesions develop on leaf petioles and main stems. Stems become brittle and snap easily, causing entire plants to collapse.
- **Tuber Symptoms:** Fungal zoospores washed from leaves into soil infect tubers, producing irregular, slightly sunken, purplish-brown dry rot on the tuber surface. Cutting the tuber reveals a dry, granular, reddish-brown to copper-colored rot extending 5–15 mm into the flesh. Secondary bacterial soft rots rapidly invade, reducing the tuber to a foul-smelling liquid mass.
- **Differentiating Feature:** The rapid water-soaked necrosis with white sporulation on the underside of leaves and lack of concentric rings clearly distinguishes Late Blight from Early Blight.

## 3. Causal Organism and Pathogen Biology
*Phytophthora infestans* is an oomycete ("water mold"), biologically related to brown algae rather than true fungi. It overwinters primarily as living mycelium inside infected seed tubers stored in cold storage, in cull piles discarded near fields, and in unharvested volunteer potato tubers left in the soil. Sporangia are produced profusely on infected foliage and spread rapidly via wind-driven rain and air currents. Optimum conditions for epidemic development are cool temperatures (10°C–20°C), relative humidity above 90%, and free leaf wetness from rain, fog, or heavy dew for at least 8 to 10 hours. Above 28°C and in dry sunlight, disease progression halts temporarily.

## 4. Prevention and Cultural Practices
- **Certified Seed Tubers:** Use only certified disease-free seed tubers procured from reliable sources (PRC Abbottabad or FSC&RD certified growers). Never use seed from fields with a known late blight infection history.
- **Destruction of Cull Piles:** Cull piles discarded along field borders and farm roads are the primary source of early spring/autumn epidemics in KP. Bury cull potatoes under at least 60 cm of soil or feed them to livestock before planting season.
- **Eliminate Volunteer Plants:** Scout fields and surrounding borders early in the season to pull out and destroy volunteer potato plants emerging from the previous season.
- **Wider Plant Spacing and High Ridging:** Plant on high, well-formed ridges spaced 75 cm apart to improve drainage, promote air movement, and create a physical soil barrier preventing zoospores from washing down to developing tubers.
- **Dehaulming (Vine Killing):** Cut and destroy potato haulms (vines) 10 to 14 days before harvest. Never harvest potatoes while green foliage is present if late blight is active, as tubers will become heavily contaminated with infectious sporangia during digging.

## 5. Integrated Disease Management (IDM) Framework
1. **Sanitation:** Eliminate all cull piles, volunteer potatoes, and infected storage residues.
2. **Preventative Monitoring:** Utilize the KP Late Blight Warning System; inspect low-lying, damp, or sheltered field areas weekly for early water-soaked spots.
3. **Preventative Sprays:** Apply protectant fungicides BEFORE disease appears whenever weather forecasts predict 2–3 consecutive days of cool, damp, overcast weather (temperatures 12°C–20°C with RH >85%).
4. **Curative Emergency Response:** If active lesions appear, immediately switch to systemic anti-oomycete fungicides to halt lesion expansion and suppress sporulation.

## 6. Treatment and Control Recommendations
- **Preventative Protective Treatments:** Apply protectant fungicides such as Mancozeb, Propineb, or Chlorothalonil on a 7- to 10-day preventive schedule during high-risk weather.
- **Curative / Translaminar Treatments:** When active lesions and white mildew sporulation are detected, immediately apply systemic or translaminar fungicide combinations:
  - Cymoxanil + Mancozeb
  - Dimethomorph + Mancozeb
  - Metalaxyl / Mefenoxam + Mancozeb (use with caution due to documented resistance in some KP districts)
  - Mandipropamid or Fluopicolide pre-mixes
- **Resistance Management:** Alternate systemic fungicides with different FRAC codes (FRAC 4, 27, 40, 43) to avoid selecting for resistant *Phytophthora* strains.
- **Regulatory Compliance:** Consult local KP Agriculture Extension officers for registered brand names, approved dosages, and legal safety advisories. Never apply unverified chemicals.

## 7. Relevant Crop-Stage Guidance
- **Emergence to Vegetative:** Scout emerging shoots for primary systemically infected stems; destroy diseased plants.
- **Canopy Closure to Flowering:** Critical protective window; the closed canopy traps humidity; initiate preventive sprays.
- **Tuber Bulking:** Maintain continuous spray coverage; ensure high soil ridging over tubers.
- **Pre-Harvest:** Dehaulm vines 10–14 days prior to harvest; wait for tuber skins to set completely before digging.

## 8. Safety, Pre-Harvest Intervals, and Environmental Notes
- Observe strict 7- to 14-day Pre-Harvest Intervals (PHI) before digging commercial tubers.
- Wear full protective equipment including eye protection, chemical-proof aprons, and nitrile gloves during fungicide handling.
- Do not spray near mountain streams, rivers, or open irrigation channels to prevent aquatic toxicity.
