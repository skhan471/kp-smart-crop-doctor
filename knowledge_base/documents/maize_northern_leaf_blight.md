# Northern Corn Leaf Blight (Exserohilum turcicum): Diagnostic Identification, Epidemiology, and Control

## 1. Disease Identification and Economic Impact
Northern Corn Leaf Blight (NCLB), incited by the dematiaceous ascomycete fungus *Exserohilum turcicum* (synonym *Helminthosporium turcicum*; teleomorph *Setosphaeria turcica*), is a devastating foliar foliar blight affecting maize in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. When epidemics establish prior to or during silking under moderate temperatures and high humidity, the widespread necrosis of upper canopy leaves severely impedes photosynthesis, resulting in 30% to 70% grain yield losses, reduced grain test weight, and structural stalk rotting.

## 2. Foliar Symptoms and Diagnostic Features
- **Early Lesions:** Initial foliar signs appear on lower leaves as small, oval, water-soaked grayish-green spots.
- **Classic Diagnostic Lesions:** As the pathogen colonizes the leaf parenchyma, lesions expand into large, elongated, spindle-shaped or cigar-shaped necrotic areas. Lesions range from 2.5 to 15 cm (1 to 6 inches) in length and 1 to 2.5 cm in width.
- **Lesion Margins and Color:** Mature lesions have tan to grayish centers with dark, wavy margins. Unlike Gray Leaf Spot (which is strictly rectangular and vein-bounded), NCLB lesions easily cross leaf veins and display pointed, tapered elliptical ends.
- **Sporulation:** In high humidity or after heavy morning dew, the lesion center becomes covered with dark olive-green to black velvety mats of fungal conidia and conidiophores.
- **Severe Blight:** When numerous lesions coalesce, large sections of the leaf blade turn necrotic, curl upward, and dry out completely, giving the field a scorched or frosted appearance.

## 3. Causal Organism and Pathogen Biology
*Exserohilum turcicum* overwinters as thick-walled dormant chlamydospores and mycelium inside undecomposed maize leaf, husk, and stalk residues. In the subsequent cropping season, as temperatures warm to 18°C–27°C with extended leaf-wetness periods of 6 to 12 hours, overwintered fungal structures sporulate profusely. Airborne conidia travel over long distances, landing on lower leaves to begin primary infections. Secondary disease cycles repeat every 10–14 days during favorable humid periods.

## 4. Prevention and Cultural Practices
- **Resistant Varieties:** Planting CCRI Pirsabak-certified resistant varieties and tolerant commercial hybrids containing Ht-resistance genes (e.g., Ht1, Ht2, Ht3, HtN) is the most sustainable and effective management tactic.
- **Crop Rotation:** Implement a 2-year rotation with non-host crops (wheat, canola, legumes, sugar beet, clover). Never follow maize immediately with another maize crop on infested ground.
- **Residue Burying:** Deep tillage using mouldboard or disc plows immediately after harvest buries infected maize stalks and leaves beneath 15–20 cm of soil, where soil microflora rapidly decompose pathogen chlamydospores.
- **Optimal Plant Spacing:** Plant maize in rows 75 cm apart with 20–25 cm intra-row spacing. Avoid high-density seed broadcasting which creates an enclosed humid microclimate beneath the canopy.

## 5. Integrated Disease Management (IDM) Framework
1. **Genetic Host Defense:** Select proven resistant hybrids certified by PARC/CCRI.
2. **Crop Rotation & Stubble Management:** Rotate fields and bury crop stubble before winter.
3. **Threshold-Based Field Scouting:** Scout maize stands weekly from knee-high through soft dough stage. If lesions appear on the lower third of plants before tasseling and rainy weather is predicted, monitor ear leaves closely.

## 6. Treatment and Control Recommendations
- **Chemical Control:** Foliar fungicides are generally reserved for seed production fields or highly susceptible high-yielding commercial stands when infection threatens the ear leaf before or at silking. Approved triazole and strobilurin active ingredients (such as Azoxystrobin, Pyraclostrobin, or Difenoconazole) provide effective control when applied at the onset of initial symptoms.
- **Application Window:** Once the crop reaches the dent stage (R5), fungicide applications yield no economic benefit and must be discontinued.
- **Regulatory Warning:** Never use unregistered pesticides. Consult KP Agriculture Extension staff at district/tehsil offices for current approved chemical registers and application rates.

## 7. Relevant Crop-Stage Guidance
- **V6 to V10 (Vegetative):** Scout lower canopy; remove volunteer plants.
- **VT to R1 (Tasseling to Silking):** Critical protection window; maintain upper canopy health.
- **R2 to R4 (Blister to Dough):** Monitor lesion progression; avoid late irrigation that creates evening leaf wetness.
- **Harvest to Post-Harvest:** Deep plow stalks; rotate with rabi wheat or berseem.

## 8. Safety, Pre-Harvest Intervals, and Environmental Notes
- Strictly observe 30-day Pre-Harvest Intervals for grain and 14–21 days for silage or green fodder.
- Wear safety respirators, boots, and gloves when preparing and spraying fungicide mixtures.
- Prevent chemical wash water from draining into livestock watering troughs or village watercourses.
